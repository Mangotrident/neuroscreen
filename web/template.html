Can a spiral and an "aaah" reveal Parkinson's?

NeuroScreen measures voice and hand tremor right in your browser. It also shows how its accuracy holds up on people it has never seen, which is where most published voice-AI numbers fall apart.

Method

What runs, and what it can't tell you

Voice pipeline

  • Audio is resampled to 16 kHz, and the steady middle of the vowel is kept (at most 4 s).
  • Pitch comes from windowed autocorrelation. Vocal-fold cycles are found by waveform matching, as Praat does.
  • Ten perturbation measures (jitter, shimmer, period variability, harmonics-to-noise ratio) feed a balanced logistic regression trained on 252 people.
  • The browser code is a line-by-line port of the Python extractor. The two agree to within 10-11 on 24 real recordings.

Spiral pipeline

  • The pen path is resampled to 100 Hz, and the best-fitting Archimedean spiral is found by searching for its centre.
  • The wobble around that ideal spiral is measured in radii, so screen size doesn't matter.
  • Tremor-band share is the fraction of that wobble's energy between 4 and 12 Hz, where Parkinsonian and essential tremor sit.
  • The spiral test shows measurements only. The validated tablet model relies on device-specific features a browser can't reproduce.

Limits

  • Trained on one Turkish clinic. Your microphone, room and compression shift every measure; uncompressed WAV is best.
  • At the default threshold the model misses many cases on new datasets. It needs recalibrating per site before any screening use.
  • Alzheimer's and stroke modules are not included. The datasets they need (DementiaBank, TORGO) require access agreements.
  • This is a research demo. It cannot diagnose anything, and a high score does not mean you have Parkinson's.
