Five everyday checks, each measured against your own normal

Voice, face, walking and two hand tests, run on-device by a home robot or by this page. Each person is compared with their own first weeks. Every accuracy claim is tested on people the models have never seen, which is where most published health-AI numbers fall apart.

2 · Hand: spiral

synthetic example

Start at the centre and draw a spiral outward, about three turns, without rushing. A finger or stylus works best; a mouse adds its own wobble.

3 · Hand: tap test

synthetic example

Using one index finger, tap the two circles in turn as fast and evenly as you can for 15 seconds. Do the right hand, then the left.

Speed, rhythm and slowing over the trial follow the finger-tapping item of the clinical Parkinson's rating scale (MDS-UPDRS 3.4). A gap between hands is the most useful single sign, because most causes affect one side first.

4 · Face: smile check

synthetic example

Look at the camera with a relaxed face for 2 seconds, then give a big smile, relax, and smile again. About 10 seconds.

Face tracking uses Google's MediaPipe Face Mesh (468 points), running on this device. These are measurements against the person's own baseline. There is no disease model, because no openly licensed labelled dataset exists to train or test one.

5 · Walking past Piko

synthetic example

Walk across the camera's view, side-on, at your usual pace, for 3 to 5 metres, with your whole body in the frame. Height is needed once, to convert pixels to metres.

Body tracking uses Google's MediaPipe Pose (33 points). Leg length (about 0.49 of height) sets the scale. Walking speed is the strongest single number here: clinicians treat under 1.0 m/s in older adults as a yellow flag for falls and frailty. Reduced or uneven arm swing is one of the earliest visible signs of Parkinson's.

Method

What runs, and what it can't tell you

Voice pipeline

  • Audio is resampled to 16 kHz, and the steady middle of the vowel is kept (at most 4 s).
  • Pitch comes from windowed autocorrelation. Vocal-fold cycles are found by waveform matching, as Praat does.
  • Ten perturbation measures (jitter, shimmer, period variability, harmonics-to-noise ratio) feed a balanced logistic regression trained on 252 people.
  • The browser code is a line-by-line port of the Python extractor. The two agree to within 10-11 on 24 real recordings.

Spiral pipeline

  • The pen path is resampled to 100 Hz, and the best-fitting Archimedean spiral is found by searching for its centre.
  • The wobble around that ideal spiral is measured in radii, so screen size doesn't matter.
  • Tremor-band share is the fraction of that wobble's energy between 4 and 12 Hz, where Parkinsonian and essential tremor sit.
  • The spiral test shows measurements only. The validated tablet model relies on device-specific features a browser can't reproduce.
  • Face and walking use Google's MediaPipe Face Mesh and Pose models (Apache-2.0) on the device. Tapping uses the two-target design of the clinical finger-tapping item.

Limits

  • Trained on one Turkish clinic. Your microphone, room and compression shift every measure; uncompressed WAV is best.
  • At the default threshold the model misses many cases on new datasets. It needs recalibrating per site before any screening use.
  • Face, walking and tapping have no disease model: no openly licensed labelled dataset exists to train or test one. They report measures and changes from the person's own baseline.
  • Alzheimer's and stroke modules are not included. The datasets they need (DementiaBank, TORGO) require access agreements.
  • This is a research demo. It cannot diagnose anything, and a high score does not mean you have Parkinson's.
